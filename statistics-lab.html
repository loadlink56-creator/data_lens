What it runs

The right test for the data in front of it.

Group comparisons

t-test, Welch's, ANOVA, Mann–Whitney, Kruskal–Wallis, or chi-square — picked from your group count, distribution shape, and variance.

Correlation and regression

Linear and rank correlation, simple and multiple regression, with coefficients, r², confidence intervals, and residual checks.

Trends and seasonality

Where a date column exists, direction, rate of change, and repeating patterns are estimated — with a note on how much history it had to work with.

Assumption checks

Normality, equal variance, independence, and sample size are tested first. When an assumption fails, the test changes and you're told why.

The part people get wrong

A p-value is not a verdict.

A tiny p-value on a huge dataset can describe an effect too small to matter. A large one on a small sample might just mean you didn't have enough rows. Data Lens always reports effect size and sample size next to significance, and writes the interpretation in words.

01

Effect size comes first

How big is the difference, in the units you actually use? That's the headline. Significance is the supporting detail, not the other way round.

02

Intervals, not just points

Every estimate ships with a confidence interval, so the uncertainty is visible instead of hidden behind a single number.

03

Many tests, adjusted

Running hundreds of comparisons guarantees some false positives. Corrections are applied and stated, so a fishing expedition doesn't read as a discovery.

04

Every choice is shown

Which test ran, which alternatives were rejected, and on what grounds — visible in the interface and recorded in the report.

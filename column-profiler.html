What it computes

The numbers you'd calculate by hand, for every column at once.

Descriptive statistics

Count, mean, median, mode, standard deviation, min, max, and every quartile — for numeric columns, and the equivalents for text and dates.

Distribution shape

A histogram with sensible bin widths, plus skew, kurtosis, and a normality check — so you know whether the mean is telling the truth.

Missing and invalid

Blanks, nulls, and values that don't fit the column's type — counted, and checked for patterns like whole ranges of rows going empty.

Outliers, with receipts

Flagged by IQR and z-score, and listed with the actual rows they came from — so you can decide whether it's an error or your best customer.

Reading a profile

A number on its own doesn't tell you much.

An average revenue of 44,976 sounds fine until you see the distribution has two humps, four missing rows, and one order worth more than the rest of the month combined. Every profile is built to show that in one look.

01

Mean and median together

When they diverge, the column is skewed and the mean is misleading. Data Lens shows both side by side and says which one to trust.

02

Bins chosen for the data

Histogram bin widths come from Freedman–Diaconis rather than a fixed count, so the shape isn't an artefact of the chart.

03

Missing values have patterns

Four blanks scattered randomly is noise. Four blanks in consecutive rows is a broken export. The profile distinguishes them.

04

Nothing is dropped quietly

Outliers are flagged, never removed. If you exclude them, that choice is recorded and shows up in the report.

What's in it

Written to be read, not decoded.

Narrative, then numbers

Each section opens with what was found, in a sentence. The tables and statistics that support it sit underneath, for anyone who wants them.

Every chart embedded

Figures are generated at print resolution with readable labels, numbered, and referenced from the text that discusses them.

Four export formats

PDF for sending, Word for editing, HTML for sharing a link, Markdown for dropping into a wiki or a repo.

Re-runnable

Drop in next quarter's files and the same report regenerates against the new data — with a summary of what changed since last time.

Who it's for

The report has to survive being forwarded.

Most analysis dies the moment it leaves the person who ran it. A report is only useful if someone three desks away can open it cold, understand what was measured, and see what to do next — without a walkthrough.

01

No jargon without a definition

Where a technical term is unavoidable, it's explained the first time it appears. Skew, IQR, and p-value each get one clear sentence.

02

Caveats stay attached

Small samples, failed assumptions, and repaired files are noted next to the findings they affect — not buried in an appendix nobody opens.

03

Reproducible by construction

Source files, row counts, settings, and version are recorded, so the same report can be regenerated and verified months later.

04

Yours to edit

Reorder sections, cut what isn't relevant, add your own commentary. The generated document is a starting point, not a locked artefact.

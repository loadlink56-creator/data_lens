What it gives you

A first draft for every column, ready to argue with.

Chosen by column type

A category gets bars. A date gets a line. Two numbers get a scatter. A single distribution gets a histogram. Each with the reasoning attached.

Small multiples

Break any chart into one panel per category, on a shared scale, so four regions can be compared without flipping between four charts.

Cross-filtering

Click a bar and every other chart on the page filters to match. Click again to clear. No query, no reload, no lost place.

Export anywhere

PNG for a slide, SVG for a designer, or straight into the report — at print resolution, with the axis labels intact.

How it chooses

Defaults you can defend.

Auto-charting is only useful if the automatic choice is usually right and always explained. Data Lens follows a short set of rules that most analysts would apply anyway, then gets out of the way.

01

Cardinality decides the form

Six categories get bars. Six hundred get a distribution instead of an unreadable axis with six hundred ticks on it.

02

Time gets a line

When one axis is a date, order carries meaning and the points get connected. When it isn't, they don't — a line between unordered categories implies something false.

03

Axes start at zero, or say so

Bar charts always start at zero. When a line chart doesn't, the axis is marked, so a 2% change never gets to look like a cliff.

04

Colour means something

One palette for categories, another for sequential values, a diverging one for anything centred on zero — and every chart stays readable in greyscale.
